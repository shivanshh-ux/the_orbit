THE DESERT PLANET

Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun – a dusty, cold, desert world with a very thin atmosphere. Mars is also a dynamic planet with seasons, polar ice caps, canyons, extinct volcanoes, and evidence that it was even more active in the past. It is about half the size of Earth, and like Earth, it has a central core, a rocky mantle, and a solid crust.

WHY IS IT RED?

Mars is known as the Red Planet because iron minerals in the Martian soil oxidize, or rust, causing the soil and atmosphere to look red. The dust that covers the surface is fine like talcum powder. Beneath the layer of dust, the Martian crust consists mostly of volcanic basalt rock. The soil of Mars also contains nutrients such as sodium, magnesium, potassium, and chloride, which are essential for life.

GEOLOGICAL GIANTS

Mars is home to some of the most spectacular geological features in the solar system. Olympus Mons is the largest volcano in the solar system, standing three times higher than Mount Everest. Valles Marineris is a system of canyons that spans a distance equivalent to the width of the United States. Mars also has two polar ice caps that grow and shrink with the seasons; they are made of a mix of water ice and frozen carbon dioxide (dry ice).

THE SEARCH FOR WATER

Scientists have found strong evidence that Mars was much wetter and warmer, with a thicker atmosphere, billions of years ago. There are features on the surface that look like ancient riverbeds, deltas, and lake basins. Today, water exists on Mars in the form of water-ice just under the surface in the polar regions, and occasionally as salty liquid water flowing down certain hillsides during the Martian summer.

EXPLORATION HISTORY

Mars is the most explored planet in our solar system (besides Earth). Since the 1960s, dozens of spacecraft have been sent to Mars by multiple countries. NASA's Mariner 4 was the first to successfully fly by in 1965. The Viking landers in 1976 were the first to successfully land and search for signs of life. Today, multiple rovers like Curiosity and Perseverance are roaming the surface, while orbiters like MAVEN and the Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter study the planet from above.

Future exploration aims to bring samples back to Earth and eventually send humans to the Red Planet. Establishing a human presence on Mars is one of the most ambitious goals of modern space agencies and private companies, potentially making humanity a multi-planetary species.
