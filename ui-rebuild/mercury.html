THE SMALLEST WORLD

Mercury is the smallest planet in our solar system—only slightly larger than Earth's Moon. It is the closest planet to the Sun, at a distance of about 36 million miles (58 million kilometers). Despite its proximity to the Sun, it is not the hottest planet; that title belongs to Venus due to its thick atmosphere.

A WORLD OF EXTREMES

Because Mercury has almost no atmosphere to retain heat, its surface temperatures vary more than on any other planet in the solar system. During the day, the Sun's rays can heat the surface to 800°F (430°C). At night, without an atmosphere to hold that heat, temperatures can plummet to -290°F (-180°C).

THE IRON CORE

Mercury is the second densest planet after Earth. It has a massive metallic core with a radius of about 1,289 miles (2,074 kilometers), about 85 percent of the planet's radius. There is evidence that it is partly molten or liquid. This core gives Mercury a magnetic field that is about 1 percent as strong as Earth's, which interacts with the solar wind.

ANCIENT HISTORY

Mercury has been known for at least 5,000 years, with sightings recorded by the Sumerians and later the Babylonians. The Greeks gave it two names: Apollo for when it appeared as a morning star and Hermes for when it appeared as an evening star. Later, the Romans named it Mercury after their swift-footed messenger god.

MODERN EXPLORATION

Because it is so close to the Sun, Mercury is difficult to visit. Only two spacecraft have ever visited the planet. Mariner 10 was the first to fly by in 1974 and 1975, mapping less than half the surface. NASA's MESSENGER mission (2004-2015) was the first to orbit the planet, revealing a world far more complex than previously thought, including evidence of water ice in permanently shadowed craters at the poles.

The current joint mission between the European Space Agency and the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency, BepiColombo, is currently en route to Mercury, with arrival scheduled for 2025 to perform even more detailed studies of the planet's composition and magnetic field.
