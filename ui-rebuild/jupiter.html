A MASSIVE WORLD

Jupiter is the fifth planet from our Sun and is, by far, the largest planet in the solar system – more than twice as massive as all the other planets combined. If Jupiter were a hollow shell, 1,300 Earths could fit inside it. It is primarily composed of hydrogen and helium, similar to the Sun. It is a gas giant and lacks a solid surface, though it may have a solid inner core about the size of Earth.

THE GREAT RED SPOT

Jupiter's familiar stripes and swirls are actually cold, windy clouds of ammonia and water, floating in an atmosphere of hydrogen and helium. Jupiter’s iconic Great Red Spot is a giant storm bigger than Earth that has raged for hundreds of years. The spot rotates counterclockwise and takes about six Earth days to complete one rotation. It has been observed since at least the 17th century.

MAGNETIC POWERHOUSE

Jupiter has the strongest magnetic field of any planet in the solar system, nearly 20,000 times more powerful than Earth's. This magnetic field creates a massive magnetosphere that traps particles from the solar wind and volcanic eruptions from the moon Io, creating intense radiation belts and spectacular auroras at the planet's poles.

A MINI SOLAR SYSTEM

Jupiter has a vast system of moons, often described as a "mini solar system." It has 95 known moons, including the four large Galilean moons: Io, Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto. Ganymede is the largest moon in the solar system, even larger than the planet Mercury. Europa is of particular interest to scientists because it is believed to have a liquid water ocean beneath its icy crust, potentially harboring life.

EXPLORATION MILESTONES

Jupiter has been visited by several spacecraft. Pioneer 10 and 11 and Voyager 1 and 2 provided the first close-up images in the 1970s. The Galileo mission (1995-2003) was the first to orbit the planet and drop a probe into its atmosphere. Today, NASA's Juno spacecraft is orbiting Jupiter, providing unprecedented data on its interior structure, gravity field, and magnetosphere.

Upcoming missions like the European Space Agency's JUICE (Jupiter Icy Moons Explorer) and NASA's Europa Clipper will focus on the Galilean moons, seeking to determine if these icy worlds have the conditions necessary to support life.
