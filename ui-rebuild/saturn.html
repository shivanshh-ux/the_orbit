THE RINGED GIANT

Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun and the second-largest planet in our solar system. Adorned with a dazzling, complex system of icy rings, Saturn is unique in our solar system. The other giant planets have rings, but none are as spectacular as Saturn's. It is a gas giant made mostly of hydrogen and helium, and like Jupiter, it has no solid surface to stand on.

A SPECTACULAR RING SYSTEM

Saturn's rings are thought to be pieces of comets, asteroids, or shattered moons that broke up before they reached the planet, pulled apart by Saturn's powerful gravity. They are made of billions of small chunks of ice and rock coated with other materials such as dust. The ring particles range in size from as small as dust to as large as mountains. The main rings are 175,000 miles (282,000 kilometers) across, but are typically only about 30 feet (10 meters) thick.

LESS DENSE THAN WATER

Saturn is the only planet in our solar system that is less dense than water. If there were an ocean big enough to hold it, Saturn would float. Its low density is due to its composition of light gases and its rapid rotation, which causes the planet to bulge at the equator and flatten at the poles—a shape known as an oblate spheroid.

THE MOONS OF SATURN

Saturn has the most moons of any planet in the solar system, with over 140 discovered so far. The largest, Titan, is the only moon in the solar system with a substantial atmosphere. It has clouds, rain, rivers, and lakes made of liquid methane and ethane. Titan is larger than the planet Mercury and is one of the most Earth-like places in the solar system, albeit at much colder temperatures.

CASSINI-HUYGENS MISSION

Our modern understanding of Saturn comes largely from the Cassini-Huygens mission (1997-2017). Cassini spent 13 years orbiting Saturn, studying its atmosphere, rings, and moons in incredible detail. The Huygens probe, which was part of the mission, successfully landed on Titan in 2005, providing the first images from the surface of a moon in the outer solar system. The mission ended in 2017 when Cassini was intentionally crashed into Saturn's atmosphere to protect its moons from potential contamination.

Saturn remains a primary target for future exploration. NASA's Dragonfly mission is planned to launch in 2027, sending a rotorcraft to Titan to explore its diverse environments and search for chemical signatures that could indicate the presence of life.
